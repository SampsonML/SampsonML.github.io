I am interested in the role of machine learning in science, and the role of physics to build more capable artificial intelligence. I approach these problems through the four main avenues below.

<div class="section-terminal">
  <div class="section-terminal-body">
    <h3 class="terminal-heading" style="margin-top:0;">Latent dynamics &amp; world models</h3>
    <p>Learning compact, physically meaningful representation spaces in which complex systems evolve, making their dynamics easier to learn, easier to constrain (if one desires), and generally more robust to systematics. On top of these I build world models that capture the generative and causal structure of physical systems, supporting intervention, counterfactual reasoning, and reliable extrapolation rather than naive pattern-matching.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="section-terminal">
  <div class="section-terminal-body">
    <h3 class="terminal-heading" style="margin-top:0;">Continual learning</h3>
    <p>Learning systems that adapt over time, accumulating knowledge rather than being retrained from scratch. I am interested in how a model can take in new data and new tasks while keeping what it already knows, and in treating learning itself as a dynamical process that can be modelled and steered.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="section-terminal">
  <div class="section-terminal-body">
    <h3 class="terminal-heading" style="margin-top:0;">AI agents for discovery</h3>
    <p>Autonomous and semi-autonomous systems that combine learned dynamics, physical priors, and reasoning to form hypotheses, design experiments, and accelerate scientific discovery.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="section-terminal">
  <div class="section-terminal-body">
    <h3 class="terminal-heading" style="margin-top:0;">Multi-agent science</h3>
    <p>Groups of agents that work the way a research group does: proposing ideas, critiquing each other's reasoning, and testing claims against data and simulation. This is a direction I am moving into, with a focus on how collaboration and disagreement between agents can make automated science more reliable than a single agent working alone.</p>
  </div>
</div>
